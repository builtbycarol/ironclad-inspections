The Prince George Homeowner's Winter Readiness Checklist

Frozen pipes, ice damming, and a furnace that picks the coldest week of the year to fail — almost all of it is preventable. Here's what to check before the temperature drops for good.

Mike Webber, CanNACHI Certified • Seasonal Maintenance
Winter Prep Homeowner Maintenance Prince George

Every fall I get calls from homeowners after something has already gone wrong — a burst hose bib, an ice dam that's found its way into an attic, a furnace that won't fire on the first cold snap. Almost everything on that list is preventable with an hour of your own time before the real cold sets in. This is the same list I run through mentally on every inspection I do this time of year, condensed into what you can check yourself.

Exterior & Envelope

CheckWhy It Matters
Disconnect and drain exterior hosesA hose left attached to an exterior bib traps water in the line, and it's one of the most common causes of a split pipe once temperatures drop below freezing — even on a "frost-free" bib.
Inspect caulking and weatherstripping around windows and doorsGaps here are direct heat loss and a path for moisture and cold air infiltration all winter long.
Clear gutters and confirm downspouts direct water away from the foundationClogged gutters back up, freeze, and contribute directly to ice damming along the roof edge.
Check roof for damaged or lifted shinglesSmall deficiencies get worse fast once snow load and ice are added — better to find them now than after a leak shows up on the ceiling.
Trim tree limbs near the rooflineSnow-loaded branches are a common cause of roof and siding damage in heavy winters.

Foundation & Crawlspace

CheckWhy It Matters
Confirm crawlspace vents are set for winter (closed or covered, per your home's design)Uncovered vents on some crawlspace designs let in cold air that can freeze exposed plumbing running through that space.
Insulate any exposed pipes in unheated spacesPipe insulation sleeves are inexpensive and take minutes to install — a burst pipe is not.
Seal foundation cracks and gaps around utility penetrationsThese are entry points for cold air and, later, meltwater during spring thaw.

Heating System

CheckWhy It Matters
Replace or clean furnace filterA clogged filter restricts airflow, forces the system to work harder, and is one of the most common causes of a mid-winter no-heat call.
Test the furnace before you actually need itRun it for a full cycle on a mild day so any issue shows up before the first real cold snap, not during it.
Check and test CO detectorsCombustion appliances working harder in cold weather make this the season CO detectors matter most — test batteries now.
Bleed hydronic baseboard heaters, if applicableTrapped air reduces heating efficiency in hot water systems.

Attic & Insulation

CheckWhy It Matters
Confirm attic insulation is even and adequate depthThin or uneven insulation creates warm spots on the roof that melt snow unevenly — a direct contributor to ice damming.
Check attic ventilation isn't blockedProper airflow keeps the underside of the roof deck cold and consistent, which is your best defense against ice dams.
Look for daylight or drafts around attic access hatchAn unsealed hatch is a surprisingly large, often-overlooked heat loss point.

Ice damming, explained. It happens when heat escaping from your living space warms the roof deck unevenly, melting snow that then refreezes at the colder eave. Water backs up under the shingles and can work its way into the attic or down an exterior wall. It's an insulation and ventilation problem before it's ever a roofing problem — which is why patching the roof alone rarely fixes it.

Why This List Matters More Here Than Almost Anywhere Else in BC

Prince George sees a real winter — sustained sub-zero stretches, real snow load, and freeze-thaw cycling that coastal BC simply doesn't deal with. Homes built or maintained with a milder climate in mind will find their weak points here first: thin attic insulation, exposed plumbing in crawlspaces, gutters that were fine for rain but not for ice. None of these show up on a home's paperwork. They show up as a call to a plumber at 11pm in January.

The Bottom Line

Most winter emergency calls trace back to one of the items on this list. None of them take more than an afternoon to check, and almost all of them are far cheaper to fix in October than to repair in January. If you'd rather have a second set of eyes go through the whole house — roof, attic, crawlspace, and mechanical systems — before the cold sets in, that's exactly what a seasonal readiness inspection covers.
