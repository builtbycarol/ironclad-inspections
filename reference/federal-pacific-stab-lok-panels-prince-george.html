Federal Pacific (Stab-Lok) Electrical Panels in Prince George Homes: What You Need to Know

By Mike Webber · Ironclad Inspections · Prince George, BC · August 2026

If you're looking at an older home in Prince George, you may hear your inspector or realtor mention a "Federal Pacific panel" or "Stab-Lok panel." It's one of the more talked-about issues in older homes across Northern BC, and it can sound scarier than it needs to. Here's a clear, practical rundown of what it actually is and what it means for you.

This guide covers what these panels are, how to recognize one, and what your options look like if your future home has one.

"A Federal Pacific panel isn't something to lose sleep over — it's something to plan for."

What Is a Federal Pacific (Stab-Lok) Panel?

Federal Pacific Electric, often shortened to FPE, was a major manufacturer of residential electrical panels from the 1950s through the 1980s. Their most common product line was branded Stab-Lok. In Canada, the related brand you'll often hear about in the same breath is Federal Pioneer, which used similar panel and breaker designs.

Millions of these panels were installed across North America, including a large number of homes here in Prince George and throughout Northern BC. The panel itself isn't dangerous just by existing — the concern is with how the circuit breakers inside are built to perform.

How to Tell If Your Home Has One

Your electrical panel is usually in the basement, a utility room, or a mechanical closet. A few visual clues can point to a Federal Pacific or Federal Pioneer panel:

What to Look For

  • A panel cover or directory labelled "Federal Pacific Electric" or "Federal Pioneer"
  • Breakers with a distinctive red stripe or red toggle switches
  • A grey panel box, often narrower than a modern panel
  • Homes built or last rewired between roughly the 1950s and 1980s

If you're not confident identifying the brand yourself, that's completely normal — it's a standard part of what gets checked and documented during a home inspection.

Why This Particular Panel Gets So Much Attention

A circuit breaker's whole job is to trip and cut power when there's an overload or short circuit — that's what prevents overheating and fire. Independent testing conducted over several decades has found that Stab-Lok breakers fail to trip properly at a notably higher rate than breakers from other manufacturers of the same era. Some of that testing has pointed to failure rates well above what would be expected from a properly functioning breaker.

Because the breaker may simply stay closed during an overload, the panel can allow a fault to keep heating up instead of shutting itself off — which is the exact scenario a breaker is designed to prevent.

Does Having One Mean Something Is Actively Wrong?

Not necessarily, and it doesn't mean the home is unsafe today. Many of these panels have operated for decades without incident. The concern is the elevated risk profile compared to a modern panel — not a guarantee of failure. Your inspector flags it the same way an old furnace or aging roof gets flagged: as a known item that deserves your attention, not a verdict on the home.

Federal Pacific Panels and Home Insurance in BC

This is usually the part that pushes buyers to act. A growing number of insurers in BC now specifically ask about Federal Pacific or Federal Pioneer panels when underwriting a policy. Depending on the insurer, this can mean a higher premium, a requirement to replace the panel within a set window before coverage is bound, or in some cases a declined policy altogether.

This is worth confirming with your insurance broker before your subject removal date, so there are no surprises after you've already committed to the purchase.

What Happens If One Is Found During Your Inspection

If a Federal Pacific or Federal Pioneer panel turns up, it gets documented in your report with photos. From there, a few practical paths forward:

Talk to your insurance broker early. Find out exactly how a specific insurer will treat the home before you're locked into the purchase.

Get an electrician's quote. A licensed electrician can give you a firm number for a full panel replacement.

Use it in your negotiation. Depending on the market and the rest of the report, this is often a reasonable point to raise with the seller through your realtor.

Plan for it. Some buyers move forward and simply budget for the replacement early in their ownership.

Replacement Costs — What to Expect

A full panel replacement is a well-established job for a licensed electrician and typically wraps up in a day. Costs vary based on panel size, amperage upgrade, and permit requirements, so getting a quote or two from local electricians is the best way to get an accurate number for your specific home rather than relying on a general estimate.

A Note for Prince George and Northern BC Buyers

Federal Pacific and Federal Pioneer panels show up regularly in Prince George homes built from the 1950s through the 1980s, including many properties in established neighbourhoods across the Bowl, Spruceland, and the Hart. It's one of the most common findings in this age of home, right alongside things like older aluminum wiring.

If you're buying anywhere in Northern BC — Quesnel, Vanderhoof, or the surrounding communities — the same housing stock and construction era applies, so it's worth having a professional check the panel regardless of which town the home is in.

The Bottom Line

Finding a Federal Pacific or Stab-Lok panel during your inspection is one of the more common "older home" findings in this region, and it comes with a clear, well-understood path forward. The goal of your inspection is to make sure you know exactly what electrical system you're buying, so you can talk to your insurer, get a real quote, and make your decision with full information instead of a guess.
